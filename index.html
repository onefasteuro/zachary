CHAPTER ONE
In the Beginning, There Was The Face 😏
👶Baby Zachy — Day One
😛The Tongue Photo — Baby Zachy
Once upon a time, on a warm August day in Florida — air thick enough to chew, sun with absolutely no chill — a tiny human arrived with dark hair, big eyes, and an expression that said: "I have assessed this situation and I have thoughts."
His name was Zachary. You could call him Zachy. Or Zack Attack. Or Zachariah — if you wanted him to slowly rotate his entire head toward you like an owl who has just been deeply, personally offended.
He is not sarcastic. Let's be clear about that. He is honest. He will tell you exactly how he feels, express every grievance with complete clarity, and then show up and help you anyway. Every single time. That is the Zachy way. That has always been the Zachy way.
And underneath all of it — underneath The Face and the honesty and the eye rolls — there is a smile so warm, so genuinely lit from within, that when it shows up it stops a room in its tracks. Not sometimes. Every time. Still does. Always will.
💛 ENDEARING FACT № 1

Zachy's smile can light up any room he walks into. He doesn't deploy it constantly — which is exactly what makes it so powerful. When it shows up for real, something shifts in the atmosphere and everyone in the vicinity feels it. The family just calls it Tuesday.

CHAPTER FOUR
The Great Outdoors: It's Complicated ☀️🚤
Zachy is a Florida boy. Born and raised in the land of relentless sunshine, humidity you can physically chew, and weather classified as "the surface of the sun but with better restaurants." And he has looked at all of it and said, with complete composure: "I'm good. I'll be inside."
Now — don't misread this. Zachy and the outdoors have a relationship. It's just entirely on his terms.
He'll wade into a river and pick up rocks the size of a golden retriever. He'll float down a stream in a bright pink inflatable tube with the confidence of a man who has genuinely zero concerns about anyone's opinion of him — a level of self-assurance most adults never reach. He'll play basketball in the neighborhood until it is so dark he cannot see the basket. He'll ride a mountain coaster through the trees with a face that says "I am completely calm and not even a little bit exhilarated right now do not look at me."

🚤 THE BOAT INCIDENT — AS IT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

Zachy went on the boat. He gave it a real, honest shot. He was out there on the water with Dad Chris, Brother Christopher, and Uncle Oliver. And he hated every single second of it.

Now — to this day — if you mention the word boat anywhere in his vicinity, he gives you that look. You know the one. Uh-uh. No way. Absolutely not. Not a full sentence. Not an explanation. Just those four words and a look that closes the conversation permanently. The man tried it. The man decided. We respect the decision.

🚗 THE DRIVING SITUATION

Zachy also tried driving. In a parking lot. With family there for moral support. He idle-rolled forward a little. Then stopped. Then idle-rolled forward a little more. Then stopped. Repeated this several times. Never left the parking lot. Never approached anything resembling actual speed. Looked at Florida traffic waiting outside. Retired from driving immediately. Honestly? Have you SEEN Florida drivers? He made the right call.

🎮 THE VR HEADSET — A LIFESTYLE CHOICE

The VR headset is not an escape. It is a better reality, carefully selected. While you're out here in regular reality dealing with regular reality problems — boats, Florida traffic, the sun — Zachy has selected an upgraded version and is thriving in it. Respect the vision. Respect the headset.

And if you're ever in the house and you hear what sounds like a young man having a very specific, very personal disagreement with a stranger from three states away — do not be alarmed. He is simply playing his video game. The same kid who barely raises his voice in regular conversation will, with full chest and zero hesitation, deliver a complete verbal performance review to a pixel-rendered stranger. It is, honestly, art.

🏔️ THE MOMENT EVERYONE REMEMBERS

Papa Mike wanted to cross the river. The water was moving. It was a real crossing. And Zachy — 14 years old — and Miki — 12 years old — each picked up a stick and flanked him on both sides.

They hyped him up. They cheered him on. They refused to leave his side until he made it across. Both of them there, sticks in hand, one on each side of Papa Mike, making sure he knew he could do it.

Papa crossed that river. And he was so proud — of himself, and of those two kids who never once left his side. That moment right there is Zachy in one perfect picture. Fourteen years old, stick in hand, not going anywhere until the person he loves makes it to the other side.

Miki — Mikayla, 17, Zachy's cousin and co-conspirator — has been causing perfectly good trouble alongside Zachy for years. The kind of trouble that makes adults nervous in the moment and proud in retrospect. Helen Georgia was their finest work.

CHAPTER EIGHT
Two Stories the Family Will Never Stop Telling 📖😂
📖 STORY № 1 — CEDAR KEY, FLORIDA: THE CEILING FAN INCIDENT

The family went to Cedar Key for a weekend trip. Nothing fancy — just the gulf, the quiet, and a rental house with, critically, a ceiling fan.

Nobody can fully reconstruct the physics of what happened next. There was Zachy — somewhere around twelve or fourteen years old. There was a pair of underwear. There was the ceiling fan. There was, presumably, some sort of throwing motion. And then there was Zachy's underwear — fully airborne, fully launched, and fully stuck on the ceiling fan. Just up there. Hanging out. New permanent resident of the rental house.

The family will tell you the story slightly differently every time. The blades were spinning. The blades were stopped. Zachy was laughing. Zachy was mortified. It is impossible to determine which version is true because everyone present was crying with laughter and no court would accept their testimony.

The underwear came down eventually. The story has not.

📖 STORY № 2 — THE BACK PORCH SLIP AND SLIDE

It was one of those Florida afternoons where the sky just opens — wind, sideways rain, the kind of storm that sends most kids inside to complain about the wifi.

Not Zachy and his cousin JonJon.

They looked out at the soaking back porch and saw, with full clarity, an opportunity. A tarp. Some soap. Whatever hose situation they could improvise. The storm itself providing free water pressure from above. And just like that — the entire back porch became a fully homemade, fully soaped-up, completely working slip and slide.

They had the time of their lives. The porch survived. Nobody got hurt. The parents had concerns. Zachy and JonJon had a perfect day, made entirely out of a day that was supposedly ruined.

Engineering school missed out. The future electrician started early.

There are more. There are always more. Ask any family member here today.
